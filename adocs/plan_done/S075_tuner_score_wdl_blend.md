## Why this exists

`tools/datagen` writes four columns and the tuner reads two of them. The third,
`score`, is the engine's own score for the position at 100000 nodes, White
relative -- and the datagen header says why it is there: "the other two are
written so a dataset can be re-filtered without replaying anything."

`adocs/eval_tuning_strategy.md` section 2.7 is the use for it:

```
target = lambda * sigma(score_from_deep_search) + (1 - lambda) * WDL_result
```

with lambda typically 0.7 to 1.0, "sometimes annealed downward during training.
Rationale: the search score is low-variance but biased toward the current
evaluator; the game result is unbiased but very high-variance. The blend beats
either alone. This is directly applicable to HCE tuning, not only to NNUE."

Today's fit is lambda 0: pure game outcome, one noisy bit spread over about 92
positions per game.

## The hazard, stated before the fit

Section 1 of the same document: "Training the eval toward its own search score at
the same depth is a fixed point that carries no information." At lambda 1 that is
exactly what this becomes, with one mitigation -- the scores in
`selfplay_v2.tsv` were produced by the engine as it was on 2026-08-14, before
S065's refit of 827 constants, so they are not the current evaluator's own
output. That mitigation expires the moment a corpus is regenerated by the engine
being fitted, and S082 and S083 both regenerate. Whoever executes this states the
generating commit next to the lambda.

## The multiple-comparison trap this step walks into

Section 7: "if you generate 20 candidate weight vectors and SPRT each at
alpha=0.05, you expect one false positive by chance." A lambda sweep is exactly
that shape. So the sweep is decided on held-out error -- which costs no machine
time worth counting, an epoch over 11.0 M rows is seconds -- and **one** vector
goes to a match. Held-out error selects; the SPRT decides. Loss improvement is
not Elo, and the audit trail should show one verdict, not five.

## Cost

Fits are minutes each. One SPRT: three to four and a half hours at the DEC-048
and DEC-050 settings.

**"Minutes each" was wrong, and the figure is measured.** A fit over the whole
11003693-row corpus at S065's budget is **0.40 s an epoch after a 55 s load and K
fit**, so a run that goes the distance to 5000 epochs is about 35 minutes and the
six-lambda sweep is around three and a half hours. That is a night's work by
DEC-041's rule, not an afternoon's, and it is still an order of magnitude cheaper
than one verdict.

## What shipped in the code

`tools/tuner_target.hpp` holds the target, and holds it as a header so a test can
reach the same functions `tuner.cpp` calls -- `tuner_split.hpp` at S066 and
`tuner_groups.hpp` at S041 are the same move for the same reason. `sigmoid` and
`LN10_OVER_400` moved out of `tuner.cpp` into it, imported back under their old
names, so the sigmoid the fit is scored through and the sigmoid the label is
built from cannot drift apart.

`--lambda F` refuses anything outside [0, 1], NaN included: `atof` answers 0.0 for
a value it cannot read, which is the pure game outcome and would look like a run
that worked.

The `score` column is parsed strictly rather than through `atoi`, which answers 0
for an unreadable field -- and 0 is a legal score meaning "the search called this
equal". At any non-zero lambda an unreadable column would be a label saying every
position is drawn, on a corpus nothing reported as broken. The whole corpus was
scanned before the column was trusted: 11003693 rows, four fields each, scores in
**-999 .. 999**, which is what makes `int16` the right store and `--quiet-limit`
1000 visible in the data.

## DEC-064, and the two questions the step file left open

The accepts fixes the shape of the run and leaves two things the arithmetic
decides. Both are DEC-064 and both are in `tuner_target.hpp` beside the code:

1. **K is fitted against the game result alone, whatever `--lambda` says.** The
   blend is built from K, so a K fitted against it fits to its own output and two
   lambda runs would not share a scale.
2. **Held-out error against the game result selects lambda, never the training
   objective.** Two lambdas train against two different targets. Measured on the
   fixture below at a deliberately wrong K of 1.5: lambda 0.7 reports held-out
   `0.033419` against lambda 0's `0.120249` on the same rows and the same split, a
   factor of 3.6 that is entirely the target moving under the metric. The tuner
   prints the game-result error as a separate `wdl` column, keeps the checkpoint
   and stops early on it, and stamps it into the emitted header.

Applying rule 2 inside a run as well as across runs is the part that needed a
choice. The alternative -- keep the checkpoint by the training objective and read
the game-result error only at the end -- is purer as an experiment and was the
first design. It was refused because the emitted vector could then sit past the
point where outcome prediction began degrading, and a lambda would lose for a
bookkeeping reason rather than for what it is. DEC-064 records it as rejected.

## The lambda 0 row is the control, and it came out as predicted

The starting constants are S065's fit, so a lambda 0 refit has almost nowhere to
go: held-out `0.118457` at the start, **`0.118454`** at its best over 5000 epochs
in 1950 s, a move of 3e-06. `apply_fit.py --dry-run` over its emitted header
places every definition and shows the drift for what it is, a handful of weights
moving by one or two.

That settles the confound this step was going to have to write down as a limit.
The candidate the SPRT plays is a refit *and* a changed label, and the incumbent
is neither, so a positive verdict could not separate the blend from the extra
5000 epochs -- except that the extra epochs are now measured at 3e-06 of held-out
error and a dozen weights of +/-1. Whatever a non-zero lambda buys or costs, more
training is not the reason.

## The verdict: the blend is not zero here, it is monotone harm

Two scans, `adocs/data/S075_lambda_sweep.log` (six lambdas, 5000 epochs, report
100) and `adocs/data/S075_lambda_fine_grid.log` (the same six, epochs 1..200,
report 5). 240 checkpoints per lambda between them. Corpus
`.tuning/selfplay_v2.tsv`, 11003693 rows, generated at `dd57c3c`, whose evaluator
is `a2f0065`'s to the constant. `--freeze tempo,piece_placement --seed 1
--validation 0.1`, K fitted at **0.7595** in all twelve runs, which is DEC-064's
first rule visible in the data.

**The incumbent's held-out error against the game result is 0.118457.** The best
any lambda reached, over every checkpoint of both scans:

| lambda | min wdl validation | vs incumbent | training-objective validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0, control | 0.118452 | -5.0e-06 | 0.118452 |
| 0.25 | 0.118530 | **+7.3e-05** | 0.074249 |
| 0.5 | 0.118838 | +3.8e-04 | 0.042760 |
| 0.7 | 0.119014 | +5.6e-04 | 0.026729 |
| 0.85 | 0.119122 | +6.7e-04 | 0.020047 |
| 1.0 | 0.119213 | +7.6e-04 | 0.017945 |

**The right-hand column is why DEC-064 exists.** It falls monotonically with
lambda, by a factor of 6.6 from end to end, and a reader ranking on it would
have shipped lambda 1.0. It means only that the 2026-08-14 engine's search score
is an easier thing for this model to predict than the outcome of a game -- which
is `eval_tuning_strategy.md` section 1's fixed point measured rather than
argued: at lambda 1 the evaluation predicts its own shallow search to a held-out
error of 0.017945.

**The noise floor came from the control and it is not assumed.** The lambda 0
run wanders between 0.118452 and 0.118494 over its first twenty checkpoints with
no trend -- the incumbent is already at the lambda 0 optimum, so what is left is
jitter, about **4e-05** peak to peak. Both of lambda 0's own "improvements", the
coarse -3e-06 and the fine -5e-06, are inside it and neither is real. Against
that floor lambda 0.25 is just under 2x and every lambda from 0.5 up is 10 to
20x, so the harm is measured rather than inferred. Rule 4 of `CLAUDE.md`: know
the resolution before believing the number.

**Every non-zero lambda degrades from its first checkpoint.** At epoch 5, wdl
reads 0.118579 / 0.118838 / 0.119014 / 0.119122 / 0.119213 for 0.25 to 1.0
against the incumbent's 0.118457, already ordered by lambda. There is no dip and
no early sweet spot: the ordering is established within five epochs and holds for
5000.

**What it does not establish.** Why. The most available explanation is the
hazard this file wrote down before the run -- the scores are the pre-S065
evaluator's, so fitting toward them pulls the weights back toward the evaluator
S065's SPRT measured at +21.10 Elo worse. That is a hypothesis and nothing here
tests it; a corpus generated by today's engine would separate "the blend is
wrong for this engine" from "these particular scores are stale". S082 and S083
regenerate, and this is the question to re-ask when they do.

## No SPRT was spent, and that was decided before the numbers

`adocs/data/S075_lambda_sweep.sh` rule 3, written before the first fit: if lambda
0 wins the selection there is no candidate, and the recorded outcome is that the
blend does not predict held-out outcomes better than the outcome alone. That is
what happened, so the three to four and a half hours a verdict costs were not
spent on a vector already measured as worse on the metric that would have chosen
it. `adocs/data/S075_sprt.sh` is committed unrun, with its pre-registered
interpretation intact, for whoever re-asks this on a regenerated corpus.

The step's accepts asks for "exactly one candidate goes to an SPRT". Zero went,
and the accepts is satisfied rather than dodged: the sweep is what selects a
candidate, and it selected none. Recording a verdict of zero without a match is
the same discipline as recording one -- S005, S006 and S015 are the precedent.

## The flag ships, at 0

`--lambda` is kept and defaults to 0, which is bit for bit the fit that came
before it. Keeping a feature whose measured value is zero needs the reason
stated, and there are three: the default path is proved unchanged, so it costs
nothing; the question it answers is not settled but conditional on a corpus whose
staleness is documented and which S082 and S083 will replace; and the flag is the
only way to re-ask it without writing the code again. What is *not* kept is any
suggestion that a blend helps -- `DEV_MANUAL.md` records the numbers above beside
the flag, so the next reader meets the measurement and not the literature figure.

## Found and fixed inside the step: anchors.py had gone stale

`.tuning/anchors.py` is the second implementation of `evaluate()` that derives the
suite's ten pinned absolute anchors, and its header states that running it against
the shipped weights must reproduce every value the suite asserts. It printed
**2 of 10**. The derivation was right and its stored expectations were not: they
were the pre-S065 numbers (88, 240, 325, 530, 1101, 266, -491) while the suite
pins 125, 211, 279, 509, 715, 224, -537 and the -446 quiescence composite. The
S065 paste updated the tests and left the script behind.

Re-pointed at the assertion that pins each value, with the file and line beside
it; **10 of 10** now. It matters to this step rather than being a drive-by: it is
the tool that derives the new anchors if a fit is pasted, and a self-check that
cries wolf would have done so in the middle of the paste. `.tuning/` is
gitignored, so the repair is recorded here and is not in a commit -- which is the
parked `status.md` item about that directory, demonstrated once more.
