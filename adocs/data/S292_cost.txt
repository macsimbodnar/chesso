#            `go depth 14`

# THE KINDS. Where the guard's condition holds (a null-move-eligible node, past the depth floor, whose entry is
# an upper bound or exact score at or below -MATE_MIN), the bound's own store is looked up by full hash:
#   P  stored by a node at which no shallow-depth rule skipped a move: a search of every legal move
#   S  stored by a node at which at least one move was skipped (futility, history, quiet SEE, capture SEE, late
#      move pruning; a skipped move that gives check is searched and does not count)
#   U  no matching store record (none in any reading on the reverted tree; one in rep_n with S289's guard in,
#      whose table differs from move to move)
# "own search" below is what the node returned when the guard stopped its pass: failed high (>= beta), not
# mated (below beta, above the band), or mated again, by a search that skipped a move or by one of every move.

== reading

1. The guard's firings are S289's in name only: 98 to 99 % of them read a bound from a node that skipped moves,
   and those bounds are not proofs -- the node's own search at the guard's depth refutes the mate behind 78 to
   81 % of them and re-proves it over every move at 0 to 0.6 %.
2. Neither kind has a tree-size cost this census can resolve. At fixed depth S's node ratio is x1.0009 over
   independent positions and x1.011 over replays, each interval spanning 1, the replay figure carried by one game
   (7076, 22.5 M nodes -> 87.5 M at ply 50); the moved rows' geometric mean is below 1 in every reading (x0.991
   to x0.999). At the game's own node budget the guard reaches deeper on average, not shallower. The firing
   nodes' own subtrees are no dearer either: 51463 nodes over 452 S firings with the guard against 54423 over
   466 passes without it (pos_d14). Whatever S289's H0 priced, it was not search effort.
3. What S does move is the game: every root best move S289's guard moved in these readings, 6 of 3000
   independent searches and 670 and 747 of 12408 replayed ones, is S's (mode 3 moves the same rows as mode 1; P
   alone moves 0, 0 and 10). Whether those moves are worse is not a node count's to say; S289's run said the
   whole guard cost (nElo -10.68 +/- 7.29), and S carries all but 1 to 2 % of what the guard changed.
4. P carries the defect. Kind P alone (mode 2) reports `mate -2` at all 31 of the defender roots S165's set
   proves mated in two (adocs/data/S289_rows.py, depth 12, `ucinewgame` per row): mode 0 25 of 31, mode 1 31,
   mode 2 31, mode 3 25; over the whole 104-row set mode 0 77 (m1 50/50, m2 25/31, m3 2/15, m4 0/8) and mode 2
   83 (m2 31/31, m3 2/15, m4 0/8) -- S289's fix's own reading (adocs/data/S289_reach.txt). The bench reads the
   same: mode 2 benches 6042933, the reverted tree's, and mode 3 6042928, S289's.
5. So the narrower form is kind P: the guard only where the bound was written by a node that searched every
   legal move. It ships as src/search.cpp's `whole_search_found_mated` with the entry's `no_move_skipped`, and
   its frozen Release build reproduces mode 2 exactly: best move, score, depth and nodes identical on 3000 of
   3000 pos_d14 rows and 12408 of 12408 rep_d14 rows.

The per-row tables are scratch (.tuning/coord/S292/census/, 11 MB; the sample rebuilds in two minutes and each
table in two to seven): what is kept is this reading. Every figure above is printed by
`S292_census.py compare` over those tables (.tuning/coord/S292/compare_all.txt) or summed from its counters.
